Who Pays After an Accident?
Understanding Compensation Rules in India

01 Jan 2025
Suri's Legal Team
8 min read

A road accident can change your life in an instant. But beyond the trauma, the question that haunts most victims is: "Who is legally responsible, and how do I get compensated?" India's legal framework provides a structured answer — but navigating it requires clarity.

The Legal Framework: Motor Vehicles Act, 1988

The primary legislation governing accident compensation in India is the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, amended significantly in 2019. Under this Act, victims of motor accidents have a statutory right to claim compensation — and the system is designed to be claimant-friendly.

Claims are filed before the Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal (MACT), which functions as a quasi-judicial body specifically constituted to adjudicate compensation disputes without the delays of regular civil courts.

Key Principle

India follows a no-fault liability principle in certain accident cases — meaning compensation may be awarded even if the victim cannot prove negligence on the part of the driver.

Who Is Liable to Pay Compensation?

Liability in accident cases is multi-layered. The following parties may be held responsible:

  • The driver — if negligence, rash driving, or traffic violation is established
  • The vehicle owner — who has a vicarious liability for the driver's actions
  • The insurance company — which is contractually bound under the third-party insurance policy
  • The employer — if the vehicle was being used in the course of employment
  • The government — in cases involving poorly maintained roads (a growing area of litigation)

Two Types of Claims: Fault vs. No-Fault

Basis Fault-Based Claim No-Fault Claim
Proof required Negligence must be proven No proof needed
Compensation amount Based on actual loss Fixed statutory amount
Legal basis Section 166, MV Act Section 163A, MV Act
Best suited for Serious injuries, high losses Quick, interim relief

How Is Compensation Calculated?

The Supreme Court's landmark judgment in National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Pranay Sethi (2017) standardized the compensation formula. Key components include:

  1. Loss of income / future earning capacity — calculated using age-based multipliers
  2. Medical expenses — all documented treatment costs, including future care
  3. Pain and suffering — non-pecuniary damages for mental anguish
  4. Loss of consortium — for the family of the deceased or severely injured
  5. Funeral and administrative expenses — in fatal accident cases
Important Update — 2019 Amendment

The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 introduced cashless treatment for accident victims at hospitals within the Golden Hour — the critical first hour after an accident. Insurance companies are now mandated to fund this regardless of fault determination.

Hit-and-Run Accidents: Is There Still Recourse?

Yes. The Solatium Fund (now restructured under the amended Act) provides compensation even when the offending vehicle cannot be traced. As of the 2019 amendment, compensation for death in hit-and-run cases was increased to ₹2 lakhs, and for grievous hurt to ₹50,000.

Claims under this scheme are filed with the Claims Inquiry Officer designated by the State Government.

The Role of Third-Party Insurance

Under Indian law, third-party motor insurance is compulsory for all vehicles. This policy covers injuries or death caused to third parties (pedestrians, passengers, other drivers). The insurance company steps into the shoes of the owner and driver — and must pay the awarded compensation.

However, insurers can deny liability if the driver was unlicensed, driving under the influence, or the vehicle was used for a purpose not covered under the policy. In such cases, the owner may be personally liable.

Practical Steps After an Accident

  1. File an FIR at the nearest police station immediately
  2. Preserve all medical records, prescriptions and bills
  3. Photograph the accident site, vehicle damage, and injuries
  4. Collect witness contact information
  5. Notify your insurance company within the prescribed timeframe
  6. Consult a legal advisor before accepting any out-of-court settlement
Caution on Settlements

Insurance companies often approach victims with quick settlement offers that are far below actual entitlement. Do not sign any discharge voucher or settlement deed without independent legal review. Once signed, it may extinguish your right to further claims.

Limitation Period

A claim before the MACT must generally be filed within six months of the accident (though tribunals have discretion to condone delay). Delay in filing weakens your claim and may result in loss of vital evidence. It is advisable to consult a lawyer as early as possible.
